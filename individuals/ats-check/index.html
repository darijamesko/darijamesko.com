This check follows fixed rules that most applicant tracking systems share. It does not use AI and cannot see design quality or the content of your experience. Treat it as a first filter, not a final verdict.

What this check looks at

  1. The CV has no readable text. This looks like a scanned image or a picture. An ATS sees an empty page, so you are out before anyone reads your name.
  2. No email address found. Recruiters cannot contact you, and many ATS forms fill in your contact details from the CV.
  3. Contact details sit only in the header or footer. Many ATS skip headers and footers, so your email or phone may simply disappear.
  4. The CV is longer than 2 pages. Recruiters skim. Page 3 is rarely read, and long CVs hide your best results.
  5. Tables or text boxes found. Many ATS read tables and text boxes in the wrong order or skip them, so dates and job titles get mixed up.
  6. The layout looks like 2 columns. An ATS reads left to right across the whole page, so 2 columns often turn into a jumble of half sentences.
  7. Photo or images found. An ATS cannot read images, and in many countries a photo can also trigger bias. Icons for phone or email hide the details.
  8. Missing standard section headings. An ATS looks for familiar headings to sort your CV. Creative headings make it file your experience in the wrong place.
  9. Only {x}% of your bullets show a number. Without numbers, your bullets describe duties, not results, and every candidate looks the same.
  10. Weak phrases. Phrases like these tell the reader what you were supposed to do, not what you achieved.
  11. No clear dates found. Recruiters and ATS filters check how long you stayed in each job. Missing dates look like something is hidden.
  12. Generic buzzwords. Every CV says this. Recruiters skip these words, and they take space from real proof.
  13. Only {x}% match with the job ad. The ATS ranks CVs by how well they match the ad. Missing words push you down the list.
