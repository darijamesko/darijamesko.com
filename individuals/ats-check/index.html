This check follows the rules most applicant tracking systems share. It does not use AI, and it cannot judge the content of your experience or the design. Take it as a first filter, not the final word.

What this check looks at

  1. Readable text. This looks like a scan or a picture of your CV. An ATS sees an empty page, so you are out before anyone even reads your name. Export your CV from Word or Google Docs as a normal PDF and check it again.
  2. Functions named clearly. This is what the ATS really searches for: your functions. In HR, for example, that means talent acquisition, payroll, compensation and benefits, onboarding, employee relations and so on. If you did the work but never name it, the system cannot find you. Name your functions in your summary, in a skills list and in your bullets.
  3. Good match with the job ad. The ATS ranks CVs by how well they match the ad, so missing words push you down the list. Where it is true for you, use the same words as the ad, in your summary and in your bullets.
  4. Email address. A recruiter who likes your CV needs to reach you quickly, and many ATS forms take your contact details straight from the CV. Put a professional email at the top of the page, in the normal text and not in a header or a picture.
  5. Length of 3 pages or less. I know it is hard to leave things out when you are proud of what you did. But recruiters rarely read beyond page 2 or 3, so your best results get lost. Describe the last 10 to 15 years in detail and sum up the rest in a few lines.
  6. Simple layout. They look neat, but many ATS read them in the wrong order or skip them, and then your dates and job titles get mixed up. A simple layout in 1 column, with normal paragraphs and bullets, is much safer.
  7. No photo or images. An ATS cannot read pictures, and little icons for phone or email hide your details from it. In many countries a photo can also bring in bias you do not need. Write everything as plain text and keep your photo for LinkedIn.
  8. Standard section headings. The ATS looks for familiar headings to understand where your experience, education and skills are. Creative headings confuse it, and then your experience lands in the wrong place. Plain headings like Work experience, Education and Skills work best.
  9. Summary at the top. Both the ATS and the recruiter start at the top. Without a short summary they have to guess who you are and what you want. Write 3 or 4 sentences under your name: your role, your years of experience, your strongest results and the job you are looking for.
  10. Bullets show what, how and the result. This is the formula I teach everyone: what you did, how you did it, and what the end result was. Numbers help, but they are not the point. "Organised trainings for new staff" tells me very little. "Introduced a 5-day onboarding programme with a buddy for every new colleague, so new staff settled in faster and stayed longer" tells me who you are and how you work. Rewrite your bullets this way, at least for your last 2 jobs.
  11. No weak phrases. Phrases like these tell me what you were supposed to do, not what you achieved. Start each bullet with what you did and what came out of it, for example: Cut hiring time from 45 to 30 days.
  12. Clear dates. Recruiters and ATS filters check how long you stayed in each job. When the dates are missing, it looks like you are hiding something, even when you are not. Add a start and end date to every job, for example 03/2021 to 06/2024.
  13. No buzzwords. Honestly, every second CV says this, so recruiters skip right over it, and it takes space from real proof. Replace each of these words with 1 short example that shows it.
