Career tips

Presenting yourself with confidence in virtual and in-person interviews

4 min read · 28 February 2025 · By Darija Meško

An interview is about so much more than the answers you give, because the way you carry yourself, the energy you bring and even the room you sit in during a video call all shape how confident and capable you seem. Feeling nervous is completely normal, and the good news is that you can still come across as calm and confident, so let me share the practical things I tell every person I prepare for an interview.

Your body speaks before you do

People read body language much faster than they process words, so before you answer the first question, your posture, your eye contact and your face are already telling a story about you.

  • Sit or stand tall with relaxed shoulders, because slouching can make you look uninterested even when you are excited about the role.
  • Smile naturally, since a warm, genuine smile makes you approachable without needing to be a big grin.
  • In person, keep eye contact natural, somewhere between staring and looking away, and on a video call look into the camera from time to time, which feels like eye contact for the person on the other side.
  • In person, use your hands naturally while you talk and offer a handshake that is firm without being a grip of steel.
  • On a video call, keep your eyes on the camera rather than on your notes, because the interviewer can tell very quickly when you are reading.

My favourite tip is to record yourself answering a few common questions and watch it back. It feels a little cringy the first time, yet it shows you exactly how you come across and which small habits you might want to change.

Your space speaks too

Your background, your lighting and your setup tell the interviewer something about you before you even say hello. I once coached someone who joined a video interview sitting on their bed with a pile of clothes behind them, and the hiring manager assumed they were not serious about the role. In the next interview the same person had a tidy background and good lighting, and the difference in how they were received was immediate.

  • Choose a quiet room and let the people you live with know that you are in an interview.
  • Sit facing a window or use a ring light, because with a bright window behind you, you turn into a silhouette.
  • Keep the background clean and calm, such as a simple wall or a bookshelf, without laundry or clutter.
  • Test your microphone and camera the day before and again 30 minutes before the call, and keep a second device ready just in case.
  • Put your phone on silent and keep a glass of water nearby.

Calming your nerves

Everybody gets nervous before an interview, so the goal is to manage your nerves rather than make them disappear completely.

Preparation is your best friend here, which means knowing the company, its values and its recent news, practising common questions with the STAR method (situation, task, action, result) and preparing a few questions of your own, because good questions make you look engaged and in control.

Right before the interview, try box breathing, where you breathe in for 4 seconds, hold for 4 seconds and breathe out for 4 seconds, a few times in a row. A short walk or a little stretching helps release nervous energy, and standing tall with your shoulders back for 2 minutes might sound silly, yet it really does lift your confidence.

For a video call, log in about 10 minutes early, and for an interview in person, arrive 10 to 15 minutes before your time, since arriving much earlier can put unnecessary pressure on the interviewer.

When you notice the nerves, try telling yourself that you are excited instead of nervous, because your body feels both in a very similar way, and that small shift can turn the same energy into something that works for you.

Remember that you are choosing too

An interview is a conversation in both directions, where you are also finding out whether this company is the right place for you, and keeping that in mind takes a lot of pressure off your shoulders.

Pick one tip from each part of this article and practise it today, whether that is your posture in front of the mirror, your interview corner at home or a few rounds of breathing. Small changes build real confidence, and I am cheering for you every step of the way.
